Change the settings

Choose which photographs Mosaic uses, how many rows each mosaic has, how long each zoom lasts, and how Mosaic Companion tells you about new versions.

Choose Mosaic Companion › Settings… (⌘,), or click Settings… under the mosaic in the Mosaic Companion window. The screen saver uses the same settings.

Source

Choose Photos library or A folder. Changing the source prepares the photographs again, from the start.

Photographs to Use

These appear when the photographs come from your Photos library.

Albums
Click Choose… to pick albums and folders, as Photos shows them in its sidebar. With none chosen, Mosaic uses the whole library; with several, it combines them. A chosen folder includes any album you add to it later.
Which photographs
When there are more photographs than Mosaic uses, this decides which ones. Spread over time takes an even share from every month of your library; Most recent takes the latest; Random takes a sample of everything.
How many
From 512 to 8,192. More photographs give mosaics more variety, and take longer to prepare and more space on disk. 2,048 is a good balance. When one of the photographs chosen can’t be used, Mosaic takes another in its place, so you get this many as long as your library has them. If Photos keeps only a small copy of some on this Mac, Mosaic uses those as tiles, and makes sure at least one in sixteen can fill the screen, as long as your library has that many.
Download from iCloud
If your library keeps originals in iCloud, this decides what Mosaic Companion may download. The setup asks you first; until you answer, it downloads nothing. With Nothing, if some close-ups turn out blurry, the Mosaic Companion window says how many, with Download Settings… to come here.
  • Nothing: Mosaic Companion uses only what Photos keeps on this Mac, and takes other photographs in place of those it can’t use. Only the photographs this Mac keeps large are shown full screen, often from a few years only, and Mosaic prefers those whose sharp copy is here; close-ups of the others are blurry.
  • Sharp close-ups only: Mosaic chooses one in sixteen of its photographs to show full screen (128 of 2,048) from your whole library, and Mosaic Companion downloads a copy of about 1.3 MB of each that this Mac has no sharp copy of, never the original. Until its copy arrives, a photograph appears only as a tile. Then it downloads a copy of the same size of some of the photographs it shows only as tiles, so that one in six of all the photographs (328 of 2,048) is sharp enough for the tiles that come on screen large around the full-screen one; these stay tiles, and come after the close-ups. At most 256 a day and 512 a week for each user of this Mac, so all of them take about two days with 2,048 photographs, and about three weeks with 8,192; none while your Mac is on a hotspot or in Low Data Mode. While it downloads, the Mosaic Companion window says how many of how many; a copy that takes longer than 15 seconds is skipped, does not count toward these limits, and is tried again later; a photograph skipped this way three times in a row is not shown full screen for a week, and another takes its place. If iCloud stops answering altogether, Mosaic stops downloading and tries again 2 minutes later, then 5, then every 10 minutes for as long as iCloud does not answer, or sooner when your Photos library changes. As long as some are still to download, the Mosaic Companion window says how many, and why. When the limit of a day or a week holds them back, Reset Limits…, in the Mosaic Companion window and here, lets Mosaic count from zero: it first says how many it will download and how much space they take, and does nothing until you confirm. Photos keeps these copies in your library until it needs the space. Below the menu, Mosaic says how many it downloaded in the last 7 days.
  • Everything needed: Mosaic Companion downloads whatever it needs to prepare your photographs, originals included. It can take a long time and a lot of space on disk: choose it only if too few photographs are ready otherwise.

Mosaic

Rows
How many rows of tiles each mosaic has, from 60 to 120. More rows mean smaller tiles and a finer picture.
Zoom duration
How long it takes to zoom out from one photograph to the next, from 10 to 60 seconds.

Updates

Whether Mosaic Companion may send notifications, which it uses only to say that a new version is ready. See Update Mosaic.

Opening at login is in the Mosaic Companion window, under In the Background. See Keep the photographs up to date.
